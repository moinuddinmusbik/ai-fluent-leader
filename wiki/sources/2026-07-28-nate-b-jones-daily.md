## The Big Idea

The skill that actually matters in 2026 is not picking the best model — it is knowing how to route work to the right model tier. Fable 5 came back after eighteen days offline with usage caps, a credit model, and a filter that reroutes some work to a weaker model. The structural lesson: we rent the intelligence, we do not own it. When any frontier model can be throttled, repriced, or pulled behind a policy line overnight, the leaders who freeze are the ones who never built a routing discipline.

## His Argument / The Framework

Nate's central claim is that "most people pick an AI model backward." They read the leaderboard, see the top name, and reach for it for everything — paying frontier prices for work a cheaper model would crush. The correct inversion is to **start with the job, not the model.**

The [[job-first-model-routing]] framework establishes three core tiers:

1. **Daily Driver (Breadth)** — For complex, ambiguous, or undefined work. Must be strong across everything because you reach for it before the scope is clean.
2. **Cheap Workhorse (Familiar/Repeatable)** — For the majority of professional work: routine decks, landing pages, memos, CRM cleanups, familiar code patterns. Nate names GLM 5.2 as the named example at a fraction of frontier cost.
3. **Frontier (Edge)** — Reserved strictly for tasks genuinely requiring frontier intelligence. Not the default.
4. **Specialists (Sensory/Action)** — When a task needs vision, live data, or agent actions, the routing shifts to the harness (which interface/connector), not the benchmark score.

The operationalization is a model-picker prompt: describe any task and it tells you which tier to open, how to check the output, and what to move off the expensive model.

## Receipts

- **Coinbase and Cursor** already stopped routing every task to the frontier — cost optimization via job-based routing is live practice at scale, not theory.
- **GLM 5.2** specifically named as capable workhorse for routine decks, memos, CRM cleanups, and familiar code at a fraction of frontier cost.
- **Model-picker prompt** — a copy-paste artifact available on Nate's Substack that routes task descriptions to the correct tier.

## Contrarian Edge

Against the consensus of "use the best model available": Nate argues that model loyalty is the failure mode, not a strategy. The benchmark leaderboard is the wrong starting point. The correct starting point is one sentence describing what the job requires. Most of what professionals do every day is "familiar and repeatable" — making the workhorse tier, not the frontier tier, the right default for the majority of work.

## What It Means — Executive Actions

1. Audit your last 20 AI tasks and classify by job type — how many were actually "familiar and repeatable"?
2. Pilot GLM 5.2 (or your chosen workhorse) on high-volume routine tasks this week.
3. Build a contingency routing map: for each task category, name the backup model if your primary is unavailable.
4. Shift selection habit: write one sentence describing the job before choosing the model.

## Mistake to Avoid

Treating the benchmark leaderboard as a model-selection framework. The top model this week and the right model for your specific task this week are two different things. When the top model gets throttled overnight, the habit becomes a liability.
