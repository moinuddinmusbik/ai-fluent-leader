# Cost-Transfer Model

Named by [[nate-b-jones]] in the episode "AI Slop Is Costing You Hours. Here's How To Stop Sending It." (2026-08-05).

## Definition

AI slop does not eliminate work — it transfers it. When a sender dispatches AI-generated content without reading it, the labor of deciding whether the document means anything is deferred to the reader. The sender saves time; the recipient pays it.

At scale: the people who read carefully are paying, in attention and time, for the people who don't.

## Evidence

- **Deloitte — A$97,587:** An unread Deloitte report reached public circulation; one external academic checked it, found errors, and the cost fell on Deloitte.
- **curl maintainer team:** AI-generated bug reports filed without reading the docs transfer hours of investigation cost to open-source maintainers.
- **Lawyers and clients:** Unread AI briefs with fabricated citations transferred legal cost and reputational risk from the lawyers to the clients.

## Implication

The cost-transfer model reframes AI slop from an aesthetic problem (bad writing) to an economic one (externalized labor). The fix is [[pro-authorship]] — a prior process that keeps the cost with the sender, where it belongs.
