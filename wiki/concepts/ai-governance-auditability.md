# AI Governance Auditability

**Definition:** The organizational capability to prove, after the fact, what an AI system did, why it did it, and whether that action was authorized — distinguished from simply having AI policies that describe what should happen.

## Key Aspects

- **The policy–auditability gap:** Most organizations have AI policies (55% per Arctera, 2026) but far fewer have the technical controls to prove compliance (only 19%). Having a policy is not the same as being able to prove you followed it.
- **What auditability requires:** Logging of AI inputs/outputs, retention of AI-assisted communications, detection and scoring of anomalous AI behavior, and a chain of custody for decisions made with AI assistance.
- **Legal exposure:** Regulatory environments (EU AI Act enforcement beginning August 2026, financial services, healthcare) require organizations to demonstrate that AI systems operated as intended — the audit trail is the proof.
- **Communications risk:** AI's integration into email, messaging, and decision documentation creates a communications risk surface that compliance teams have not historically had to govern.
- **Sectors most exposed:** Finance, healthcare, and energy/utilities face the steepest compliance exposure as AI embeds into customer-facing and decision-critical communications.
