# Pro-Authorship

Named by [[nate-b-jones]] in the episode "AI Slop Is Costing You Hours. Here's How To Stop Sending It." (2026-08-05).

## Definition

Pro-authorship is the discipline of owning everything you send — including reading it, meaning it, and standing behind it — before it reaches the recipient. It is not a style rule or a tone-of-voice checklist. It is a prior human act that determines whether the work is worth sending at all.

## Core Principle

"If you didn't read it, don't send it to me." — Nate B. Jones's named personal standard.

The principle locates accountability with the sender, not the model or the tool. When a document causes harm — financial, reputational, legal — the cost follows the sender, not the generator. Pro-authorship makes this explicit before the send, not after.

## Components

1. **The Accountability Standard** — A named personal principle that treats reading as a prerequisite for sending. Without a named standard, accountability defaults to nobody.
2. **The [[voice-discovery-skill]]** — A structured process for capturing your authentic voice as a reference artifact before prompting AI. Steers the model toward your perspective rather than the [[hill-climbing-convergence]] mean.

## Why Checklists Cannot Substitute

Anti-slop checklists (avoid "delve," vary sentence length, add personality) describe surface features of a desired output. They don't address the architectural cause — [[hill-climbing-convergence]] — and they don't ask whether the sender has done the reading. A checklist is a downstream edit; pro-authorship is a prior process.
