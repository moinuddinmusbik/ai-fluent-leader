# AI Slop Is Costing You Hours. Here's How To Stop Sending It.

**Lens:** FRAMEWORK | **Date:** 2026-08-05 | **YouTube · Substack**

## The Big Idea

AI-generated work doesn't eliminate effort — it transfers it invisibly to whoever reads the document next. Nate's argument: we've solved the blank page but created something worse — acres of finished-looking documents that nobody checked, traveling at machine speed to readers still expected to pay attention and act. The fix is not a better prompt, a longer checklist, or a stricter style guide. It is a discipline he calls **pro-authorship**: owning everything you send, before it leaves your hands.

## The Framework: Pro-Authorship

- **The Core Principle:** "If you didn't read it, don't send it to me." Nate names this as his personal professional standard — and the only anti-slop measure that is structurally sound, because it locates accountability with the sender rather than the model.
- **The Voice Discovery Skill:** A structured process for identifying your authentic voice *before* prompting. The output becomes a reference that steers the model toward your perspective rather than toward the convergence mean — the only thing rivals can't buy from the same API.
- **Authorship as a Process, Not a Hill:** Authorship isn't a quality level the model can reach — it's a prior human act: deciding what you mean, why it matters to the recipient, and whether you'd stand behind it if asked.

## How It Works

- **The Cost-Transfer Mechanism:** AI slop does not reduce work — it defers it. The document looks finished; the labor (deciding whether it means anything) has been handed to the reader.
- **Hill-Climbing Convergence:** All LLMs optimize toward reward signals representing "good writing." Every model climbs the same hill — polished, inoffensive, increasingly identical. This convergence is architectural, not a flaw fixable by switching models.
- **Why One Anti-Slop Checklist Fails:** Checklists describe surface features of the destination but don't change the model's trajectory. One universal checklist produces one universal voice — deepening convergence rather than curing it.
- **What the Voice Discovery Skill Does:** By capturing your distinctive voice as a reference artifact *before* prompting, you shift the model's starting point from the generic mean to your perspective.

## Where It Applies

- Any document with a downstream decision: performance reviews, risk assessments, strategy decks, pricing memos, hiring decisions.
- AI-scaled teams, where one person's unread output becomes another person's unread input — the cost-transfer compounds.
- Career-sensitive work: in an environment of converging AI voices, a distinctive and accountable voice becomes a professional signal that cannot be automated away.

## Receipts

- **Deloitte — A$97,587 refunded:** A Deloitte report circulated before anyone checked its numbers. One external academic did — and found errors. A$97,587 refunded.
- **curl maintainer team — hours lost per AI bug report:** Developers paste AI-generated bug reports into the curl project without reading the docs. Each fabricated report costs maintainers hours.
- **Lawyers fined by a judge:** A judge sanctioned lawyers for submitting AI-generated briefs with fabricated citations. Cost fell on clients — not the tool.
- **Nate's own drafting process:** Dozens of drafts before and after AI. Speed changed; readiness is a separate variable.

## What It Means For You

1. **Before your next send, do the read.** Adopt a named standard — "if I didn't read it, it doesn't leave" — and say it out loud.
2. **Build a Voice Discovery process before scaling AI output.** Collect 3–5 authentic samples of your own writing; use them as reference when prompting — a model of your perspective, not a word-avoid list.
3. **Measure AI productivity in downstream attention cost, not upstream output speed.**
4. **Name your standard explicitly.** Accountability requires a named principle. The Deloitte and lawyer cases share the same failure: no one was explicitly responsible for the read before the send.

## Mistake to Avoid

Treating AI slop as an aesthetic problem fixable with a checklist. Swapping "delve" for "examine" doesn't change the architectural convergence or who bears the attention cost. The fix is authorship (a prior process), not editing (a downstream fix).
