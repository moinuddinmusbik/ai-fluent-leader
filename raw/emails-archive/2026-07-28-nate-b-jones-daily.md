## The Big Idea

The skill that actually matters in 2026 is not picking the best model — it is knowing how to route work to the right model tier. Fable 5 came back after eighteen days offline with usage caps, a credit model, and a filter that reroutes some work to a weaker model. The lesson is not about Fable specifically; it is structural: we rent the intelligence, we do not own it. When any frontier model can be throttled, repriced, or pulled behind a policy line overnight, the leaders who freeze are the ones who never built a routing discipline. The ones who keep working already know where each job goes next.

## The Framework: Job-First Model Routing

- **Start with the job, not the model.** The first question before any AI task is not "which model?" — it is "what does this task actually require?" Model selection follows job classification, not benchmark rankings.
- **Daily Driver (Breadth tier).** Your go-to when the job is not yet clean — complex, ambiguous, high-stakes, or cross-domain. Must be strong across everything because you reach for it before the scope is defined.
- **Cheap Workhorse (Familiar/Repeatable tier).** Earns its spot once the work is familiar and repeatable: routine decks, landing pages, memos, CRM cleanups, code with a familiar shape. The majority of professional work falls here. Nate cites GLM 5.2 as an example at a fraction of frontier cost.
- **Frontier (Edge tier).** Reserved strictly for tasks that genuinely require frontier intelligence — novel reasoning, high-ambiguity synthesis, or capabilities unavailable at lower tiers. Not the default.
- **Specialists (Sensory/Action tier).** Once a job needs vision, live data, or agent actions, routing shifts to the harness design — which interface, not which benchmark score.

## How It Works

- **Classify the task first.** Repeatable → Workhorse. Novel/undefined → Daily Driver. Genuinely frontier-requiring → Frontier.
- **Use the model-picker prompt.** A single copy-paste prompt that takes a task description and routes it to the right tier — deck, repo, or call — available on Nate's Substack.
- **Build redundancy into the routing.** Know in advance where each job type goes if your first-choice model is unavailable. Routing discipline means the work keeps moving when a model is throttled.
- **Revisit the classification periodically.** As models get cheaper, work that required the frontier six months ago may now be workhorse territory.

## Where It Applies

- **Enterprise-scale operations.** Coinbase and Cursor already made the routing move — stopping the practice of sending every task to the frontier.
- **Daily individual workflows.** For a professional with thirty minutes before the next meeting, the routing question is immediate: which AI to open first.
- **Agentic and multimodal pipelines.** When tasks need vision, retrieval, or actions, routing shifts from model quality to harness design.
- **Model-market volatility planning.** The Fable 5 outage is the stress test: job-first routing gives you a pre-built contingency map.

## What It Means For You

1. **Audit your AI usage this week.** Pull your last 20 AI-assisted tasks and classify each by job type. If the majority are "familiar and repeatable," you are likely paying frontier prices you do not need.
2. **Route your repeatable work to a cheaper model.** Pilot GLM 5.2 (or your chosen workhorse) on high-volume routine tasks: meeting summaries, standard slides, CRM updates, template-based code.
3. **Build your contingency routing map.** For each task category, name the backup: if your primary model is unavailable tomorrow, where does that work go?
4. **Shift your selection habit from leaderboard to job description.** Before your next AI task, write one sentence describing what the job requires — then choose the model.

## Mistake to Avoid

Using the benchmark leaderboard as your model-selection framework. The model at the top of the leaderboard this week and the model right for your specific task this week are two different things. Most people pick an AI model backward — starting with the model name rather than the job. When that model gets throttled or repriced overnight, the habit becomes a liability.
