## You Can Hand One AI Agent Your Worst Recurring Task. It Cleared 60% Of Mine.

### The Big Idea

The first AI agent job at your organization shouldn't be a new capability — it should be the repair loop your team is already running by hand. When Nate sorted 52 customer-support tickets by root cause instead of subject line, he found that 39 of 52 were the same Slack-access failure repeated. His team had achieved 98% ticket closure and called it a great week — without noticing they were efficiently cleaning up a mess they kept making. The core argument: don't automate the answer. Fix the path that keeps breaking. Agents make that possible at scale, but only if leaders look for the repeating pattern first.

### The Framework: Root-Cause First, Then Agent

1. **Pull 50–100 recent tickets (or operational cases):** Support queue, IT access log, finance exceptions — wherever your team keeps fixing the same thing by hand.
2. **Strip PII, then group by root cause — not subject line:** What actually failed? This is the diagnostic pivot.
3. **Pick the largest root-cause category as the agent's first job:** Boring and reversible wins.
4. **Write the process down before automating anything:** The documentation IS the agent's instruction set.
5. **Keep human approval on exactly two things:** Access decisions and money.
6. **Scorecard it next week:** Count how many times the same category occurred. Dropping number = fixed cause.

### How It Works

- **The hidden cost isn't the reply — it's the reconstruction:** Reconstructing the customer's state across half a dozen systems is the expensive part. The reply is cheap.
- **The agent owns the research loop, humans own the judgment calls:** Agent prepares recommendation; human decides on access and money.
- **From 52 cases to 19 in a comparable week:** Upstream fix shrank the category, not just closure speed.
- **Gumroad's agent went further:** Found a charting bug, wrote the test, shipped the fix — then got the design wrong. Customer approval was required.

### Where It Applies

- **Customer support:** Sort by root cause; fix broken path. 52→19 cases/week.
- **Finance:** Invoices without purchase orders — same repeating exception.
- **IT/software access:** Recurring access requests from a broken onboarding path.
- **Sales:** Account history research before every call — agent carries the reconstruction.
- **Product:** Ten people reporting one bug — root-cause grouping surfaces single upstream issue.

### Receipts

- 51 of 52 support issues closed with AI — 98% closure rate masked 39 identical Slack-access failures.
- 52 cases → 19 in a comparable week — 63% reduction by fixing upstream path.
- 26 patterns identified; 2 upstream failures — the root-cause sort produced 26 distinct categories, 2 needed upstream fixes.
- Gumroad case study: agent found charting bug, wrote test, shipped fix; customer caught design error.

### What It Means For You

1. **Run the root-cause sort this week.** Pull last 50 tickets. Strip names. Group by what failed. Biggest group = agent's first job.
2. **Write the process before you touch automation.** Documentation-first is the instruction set.
3. **Draw the two bright lines: access and money.** Everything else the agent can prepare. Set this as policy before deploying.
4. **Change your scorecard metric.** Stop measuring tickets closed. Start measuring how many times the same root-cause category occurred week-over-week.

### Mistake to Avoid

Measuring agent success by closure rate instead of recurrence rate. 98% closure (51/52) masked 39 repeating instances of the same broken path. The metric that matters: did this category appear less this week than last?
